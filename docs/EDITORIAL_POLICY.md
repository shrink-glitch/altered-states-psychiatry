## Editorial policy

### Aims
To help trainees assess religious, spiritual and psychedelic-related presentations with respect for patients' frameworks, and without missing treatable illness. The stance follows the WPA Position Statement on Spirituality and Religion in Psychiatry (2016).

### Review process
Every case passes through three stages, shown by its `status` field:

1. **draft** - written by an author; visible on the site with a "not yet peer reviewed" label.
2. **published** - reviewed by at least **two** psychiatrists, at least one of them a consultant, who are not authors of the case. Reviewers are named on the case page.
3. **hidden** - withdrawn pending correction.

Reviewers check:
- clinical accuracy and consistency with current DSM-5-TR and ICD-11
- that the "best" answer is defensible and the feedback for other options is fair
- that every factual claim is supported by a reference in `content/references.bib`
- cultural accuracy, preferably with input from someone familiar with the tradition depicted
- that no real patient could be identified

### Standards
- Cases are fictional composites. No real patient details, even disguised.
- Spiritual and religious traditions are described respectfully and accurately. Cases neither endorse nor dismiss metaphysical claims.
- No content on obtaining, dosing or using controlled substances.
- Every reference is checked against PubMed or the publisher before merging.
- Each case shows the month it was last reviewed. Cases are re-reviewed every 2 years.

### Conflicts of interest
Authors and reviewers declare any relevant financial or professional interests (for example, working in a clinic that offers ketamine or psychedelic-assisted therapy) in the pull request.
